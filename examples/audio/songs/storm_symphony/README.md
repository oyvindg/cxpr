# Storm Overture

`Storm Overture` is an original thirty-two-bar orchestral movement inspired by the
dramatic minor-key energy of the early Romantic period. It uses a compact
rhythmic cell, driving low strings, forceful brass, timpani punctuation and
contrasting woodwind answers. It does not reproduce a Beethoven composition.
The principal idea follows the motivic economy and short-short-short-long
rhetoric associated with Beethoven's Fifth while using original pitches,
harmony and orchestration.
The second sixteen-bar span uses a new harmonic sequence, an inverted and
expanding ostinato, rising motivic pitches, denser woodwind exchanges and a
larger final tutti rather than simply repeating the exposition. Fast
sixteenth-note string arpeggios enter selectively during the development.

The mix places each section on an orchestral stage and combines early chamber
reflections with a dark stereo hall. Sustained instruments use independent,
instrument-specific vibrato and sample-continuous envelopes.
The movement opens with a dedicated low timpani stroke feeding a long stereo
hall before the principal motif enters.
